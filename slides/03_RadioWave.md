# **Chapter 3: Radio Wave Principles**
### The Invisible Highway

---

## **Section 3.1: The Radio Wave**

### What is a Radio Wave?
* An AC signal sent into space.
* Consists of **Electric (E)** and **Magnetic (H)** fields traveling together.
* **Frequency (f):** How many cycles per second.
  * Unit: **Hertz (Hz)**.
* **Wavelength ($\lambda$):** The physical distance the wave travels in one cycle.



---

## **The Math: Frequency vs. Wavelength**

### The Relationship
* As Frequency goes **UP**, Wavelength goes **DOWN**.
* **The Magic Number:** 300
* **Formula (for meters and MHz):**
  * $\lambda \text{ (meters)} = \frac{300}{f \text{ (MHz)}}$
  * $f \text{ (MHz)} = \frac{300}{\lambda \text{ (meters)}}$

**Example:**
* What is the wavelength of a 146 MHz signal?
* $300 \div 146 \approx \mathbf{2 \text{ meters}}$ (The 2-meter band!)

---

## **Section 3.2: The EM Spectrum**

### Organizing the Airwaves
* **RF (Radio Frequency):** A specific slice of the Electromagnetic Spectrum.
* **Hertz (Hz) Scale:**
  * **1 Hz** = 1 cycle/sec
  * **1 kHz** (Kilohertz) = 1,000 Hz
  * **1 MHz** (Megahertz) = 1,000,000 Hz
  * **1 GHz** (Gigahertz) = 1,000,000,000 Hz



---

## **Section 3.3: Ham Radio Bands**

### Key Frequency Ranges (Exam Focus)

| Range | Name | Frequency | Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **HF** | High Frequency | 3 – 30 MHz | Long distance (World) |
| **VHF** | Very High Freq | 30 – 300 MHz | Local / Regional |
| **UHF** | Ultra High Freq | 300 – 3000 MHz | Local / Short Range |

* **Technicians** have full access to VHF and UHF.

---

## **Section 3.4: How Signals Travel**

### 1. Line of Sight (Direct Wave)
* Primary mode for **VHF/UHF**.
* The radio sees the antenna.
* Limited by the **Radio Horizon** (slightly further than visual horizon).

### 2. Sky Wave (Skip)
* Primary mode for **HF**.
* Signals bounce off the **Ionosphere** and return to Earth.
* Allows for worldwide communication.



---

## **Other Propagation Factors**

* **Radio Horizon:** VHF/UHF signals travel ~15% further than your eyes can see.
* **Multipath:** Signal reflects off buildings/hills, causing "picket fencing" (flutter) on mobile signals.
* **Picket Fencing:** The rapid fluttering sound on a signal as you move.
* **Sporadic E:** Occasional intense ionization allowing long-distance VHF (6m/10m) communication.

---

## **Section 3.5: Modulation**

### Adding Voice to the Carrier
* **CW (Continuous Wave):** Morse Code. Narrowest bandwidth.
* **AM (Amplitude Modulation):** Signal strength varies. Susceptible to noise.
* **SSB (Single Sideband):** A form of AM. Efficient voice mode for HF.
  * **LSB:** Lower Sideband (Below 10 MHz - 40m, 80m, 160m).
  * **USB:** Upper Sideband (Above 10 MHz + VHF/UHF).
* **FM (Frequency Modulation):** Frequency varies. Best sound quality, used on VHF/UHF.



---

## **Bandwidth**
* The "width" of the signal on the spectrum.
* **Narrower is efficient; Wider is higher quality.**

| Mode | Bandwidth |
| :--- | :--- |
| CW | ~150 Hz |
| SSB | 2 - 3 kHz |
| AM | 6 kHz |
| FM | 10 - 15 kHz |

---

## **Section 3.6: Digital Modes**

### Computer-to-Computer Radio
* **Packet Radio:** Data sent in bursts (packets).
* **APRS (Automatic Packet Reporting System):** Uses GPS to track location.
* **DMR / D-STAR / Fusion:** Digital voice modes on VHF/UHF.
* **FT8:** Weak signal mode for making contacts in poor conditions.

---

## **Chapter 3 Review**

### Key Takeaways
1. **Wavelength** is $300 \div \text{Frequency (MHz)}$.
2. **VHF/UHF** are "Line of Sight".
3. **HF** uses the Ionosphere ("Skip").
4. **USB** for VHF/UHF voice; **LSB** for HF below 40m.
5. **FM** is the primary mode for local Technicians.
