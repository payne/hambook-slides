# **Chapter 4: Antennas**
### The Mouth and Ears of Your Station

---

## **Section 4.1: Antenna Basics**

### Resonance
* An antenna works best when its physical length matches the radio wave's length.
* **Resonant:** The antenna is naturally tuned to the frequency.
* **50 Ohms:** The magic impedance number for Ham Radio.

[Image of dipole antenna diagram]

---

## **Common Antenna Types**

### 1. The Half-Wave Dipole
* Two wires extending in opposite directions.
* Total length is usually **1/2 wavelength**.
* **Pattern:** "Figure 8" (radiates broadside to the wire).

### 2. The Quarter-Wave Vertical
* Single radiating element standing up.
* Requires a **Ground Plane** (radials or a car roof) to act as the "missing" half of the antenna.
* **Pattern:** Omni-directional (equal in all directions).



---

## **Antenna Math**

### Calculating Length (in feet)
* **Half-Wave Dipole:**
  * $L = 468 \div \text{Frequency (MHz)}$
* **Quarter-Wave Vertical:**
  * $L = 234 \div \text{Frequency (MHz)}$

**Example (2-Meter Band):**
* $234 \div 146 \text{ MHz} \approx \mathbf{1.6 \text{ ft}}$ (~19 inches)
* This is why most handheld antennas are about 19 inches long (or shortened with coils).

---

## **Section 4.2: Polarization & Gain**

### Polarization
* The orientation of the Electric Field.
* **Rule:** Antenna orientation determines polarization.
  * Vertical Antenna $\rightarrow$ Vertical Polarization.
  * Horizontal Wire $\rightarrow$ Horizontal Polarization.
* **Mismatch:** If you use Vertical to talk to Horizontal, signal loss is massive.
* **VHF/UHF:** Mostly Vertical (Repeaters/Cars).
* **HF:** Mostly Horizontal (Dipoles).

---

### Directional Antennas (Beams/Yagis)
* Focuses energy in one direction (like a flashlight).
* **Gain:** The apparent increase in power due to focusing.
* **Yagi Antenna:** Has a Reflector (back), Driven Element (feed), and Directors (front).



---

## **Section 4.3: Feed Lines**

### Getting Signal to the Antenna
* **Coaxial Cable (Coax):** The standard for Ham Radio.
* **Impedance:** Must be **50 Ohms** (TV cable is 75 Ohms - don't use it!).
* **Loss (Attenuation):**
  * All cable loses signal (turns it into heat).
  * Higher Frequency = **More Loss**.
  * Longer Cable = **More Loss**.

**Common Types:**
* **RG-58:** Thin, flexible, high loss (good for short mobile runs).
* **RG-8 / RG-213:** Thick, heavy, low loss (good for base stations).

## **Section 4.4: Connectors**

### The Weakest Link
* Most failures happen at the connectors. Waterproofing is critical!

| Type | Use Case | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **PL-259 (UHF)** | HF / Mobile | "Old faithful," bad for >400 MHz |
| **N-Type** | UHF / Base | Waterproof, low loss at high freq |
| **SMA** | Handhelds | Small, delicate |
| **BNC** | Portable | Quick-disconnect twist |



---

## **Section 4.5: SWR**

### Standing Wave Ratio
* A measure of how well the antenna matches the transmitter.
* **1:1** = Perfect match (All power goes out).
* **High SWR:** Power is reflected back to the radio (Heat).
  * **> 2:1:** Acceptable but not great.
  * **> 3:1:** Protection circuits may reduce power to prevent damage.

**Tools:**
* **SWR Meter:** Measures match.
* **Antenna Tuner:** "Fools" the radio into seeing a 1:1 match (does *not* fix the actual antenna efficiency).

---

## **Chapter 4 Review**

### Key Takeaways
1. **Dipole** length = $468 \div \text{Freq}$.
2. **Vertical** length = $234 \div \text{Freq}$.
3. **VHF/UHF** is usually **Vertical** polarization.
4. **Coax** gets "lossier" as frequency goes up.
5. **SWR** above 2:1 implies a mismatch; check your connections!
