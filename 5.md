# **Chapter 5: Safety**
### Don't Be the Path to Ground!

---

## **Section 5.1: Electrical Shock**

### The Danger
* **Voltage** pushes current through the body.
* **Current** disrupts the heart and burns tissue.
* **Low Voltage $\neq$ Safe:** Even 30 Volts can be dangerous under the right conditions.
* **The Path:** Current creates a complete circuit. **Don't let your body be part of the circuit.**

### Prevention
* Keep one hand in your pocket when working on high voltage (prevents path across the chest).
* Disconnect power *before* working on equipment.
*Discharge capacitors (they hold charge even when unplugged).*



---

## **Section 5.2: Wiring & Grounding**

### AC Wiring Colors (US Standard)
* **Black:** Hot (Live)
* **White:** Neutral
* **Green (or Bare):** Safety Ground

### DC Wiring Colors (Radio Standard)
* **Red:** Positive (+)
* **Black:** Negative (-)

> **Exam Tip:** Do not confuse AC "Black" (Hot) with DC "Black" (Negative)!

---

## **Section 5.3: Fuses & Breakers**

### Protection Devices
* **Purpose:** To prevent **fire** caused by overheated wires.
* **Placement:** Always install the fuse **as close to the power source (battery) as possible**.
* **Why?** If the wire shorts out *before* the fuse, the wire will burn. If the fuse is at the battery, the fuse blows first.



### GFCI (Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter)
* Detects imbalances between Hot and Neutral current.
* **Use:** Anywhere water is present (kitchens, bathrooms, outdoor shack).

---

## **Section 5.4: Battery Safety**

### Lead-Acid Batteries (Car Batteries)
* **Hazard 1:** Explosive Hydrogen Gas during charging (needs ventilation).
* **Hazard 2:** Acid spills.
* **Hazard 3:** High Current (shorting terminals can melt tools/rings).

### Lithium-Ion / LiFePO4
* **Hazard:** Fire if punctured, overcharged, or overheated.
* Always use the charger specifically designed for that battery chemistry.

---

## **Section 5.5: Antenna Safety**

### Power Lines
* **The Golden Rule:** Look UP and LIVE.
* Keep antennas at least **10 feet** away from power lines (more is better).
* Assume **ALL** lines are energized.
* If an antenna falls on a power line: **DO NOT TOUCH IT.** Call the power company.

### Tower Climbing
* Never climb without a climbing harness.
* Never climb alone.
* Never climb a crank-up tower without locking it in place.



---

## **Section 5.6: RF Exposure**

### Is Radio Hazardous?
* **Ionizing Radiation** (X-Rays, Nuclear): Damages DNA.
* **Non-Ionizing Radiation** (Radio): **Heats tissue.**
* **The Danger:** RF burns (internal heating). The eyes and reproductive organs are most sensitive (less blood flow to cool them).

### Maximum Permissible Exposure (MPE)
* **Controlled Environment:** You know the hazard and can avoid it (Your station).
* **Uncontrolled Environment:** General public (Neighbors). Limits are stricter here.



---

## **Duty Cycle**

### Time Averaging
* RF exposure is calculated based on "average" exposure over time.
* **Duty Cycle:** The percentage of time you are actually transmitting.
* **Example:**
  * **FM Voice:** High Duty Cycle (you talk a lot).
  * **SSB Voice:** Low Duty Cycle (pauses in speech, no carrier).
  * **CW (Morse):** ~40% Duty Cycle.
* **Higher Duty Cycle = Lower Power Limit** to stay safe.

---

## **Chapter 5 Review**

### Safety Checklist
1. **Fuse** the red wire close to the battery.
2. **Green** is ground (AC); **Red** is positive (DC).
3. **10 Feet** clearance from power lines.
4. **RF Energy** heats tissue; it does not cause genetic damage.
5. **Ventilate** lead-acid batteries.
